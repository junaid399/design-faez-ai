What to bring, and what to do first.

Six small things that make check-in take two minutes instead of fifteen.

  • National ID or Iqama

    Needed at every visit. Children are registered on a parent's file.

  • Insurance card

    The plastic card or the digital card in your insurer's app both work.

  • Reports and medicines

    Bring recent results, scans and the boxes of any medicine you take.

  • Fasting for blood tests

    Sugar and cholesterol tests need 8 to 10 hours without food. Water is fine.

  • Ten minutes early

    First visits include a short registration at the kiosk in the lobby.

  • Parking and access

    Free basement parking with lift access. Wheelchairs wait at the entrance.

Insurance and payment, explained plainly.

With insurance

We bill most Saudi insurers directly. You pay only the co-payment written on your card, usually between 0 and 20 percent of the visit.

  • Cover is checked at the kiosk before you see the doctor
  • Tests and scans may need a separate approval
  • Your insurer's name is shown on the receipt

Paying yourself

Consultation prices are on the screen at reception and on your receipt. Ask for the price of any test or procedure before it is done.

When a test needs your insurer's approval

  1. During the consultation

    Your doctor sends the request

    It goes to your insurer electronically, with the reason for the test.

  2. Usually 15 to 60 minutes

    Your insurer reviews it

    You can wait in the lounge or go home. We send the answer by SMS.

Approved

The test is done the same day, at no extra cost beyond your co-payment.

Not approved

We explain why and give you the self-pay price before anything is done.

When coming in is the hard part.

Home visits

A nurse can come to you in north Riyadh for injections, wound dressing and blood samples. Doctors visit older patients who cannot travel.

Video consultations

Follow-ups, results and repeat prescriptions over a 15-minute video call in the Nabd app, from 8 am to 11 pm.

Questions patients ask us.

Still not sure? Reception answers calls and WhatsApp messages during opening hours.

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. You can book any department directly. A few insurance policies ask for a family medicine visit first, and reception will tell you at check-in if yours does.

Can I ask for a woman doctor?

Yes. Five of our eleven doctors are women, covering every department except physiotherapy, which offers women-only sessions with a female therapist.

How do I get a sick leave certificate?

If your doctor agrees you need it, sick leave is issued through Sehhaty before you leave. Your employer can verify it there.

Are prescriptions sent to a pharmacy?

Yes. Prescriptions go through Wasfaty, so you can collect them from our pharmacy or any pharmacy in the network.

What happens if I am late?

We hold your appointment for 15 minutes. After that, reception fits you into the next free slot with the same doctor or offers another doctor.

How do I cancel or change an appointment?

Reply to the confirmation SMS, change it in the app or call reception. There is no charge if you tell us at least two hours before.

Can I bring my children to my own appointment?

Yes, there is a supervised play corner next to the women's lounge. If a child has a fever, tell reception so they can wait in the paediatric area.

Do you treat emergencies?

No. Nabd is an outpatient complex without an emergency room. In an emergency call 997 for the Saudi Red Crescent or go to the nearest hospital emergency department.
