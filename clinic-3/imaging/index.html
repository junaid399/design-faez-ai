Four ways of looking inside, and when each is used.

Magnetic resonance imaging

Magnets and radio waves, no radiation. The clearest pictures of soft tissue: ligaments, cartilage, discs, nerves, the brain and heart muscle.

In the scanner
30 to 45 minutes, 60 for cardiac MRI
Report
Within 48 hours, double read
Before you come
Tell us about pacemakers, implants, metal fragments or pregnancy. Wear clothes without metal. Fast 4 hours only for abdominal scans.

Computed tomography

Fast X-ray slices for the chest, abdomen, sinuses, bones and heart arteries. Our low-dose protocols cut radiation by about a third.

On the table
About 10 minutes, a few seconds of scanning
Report
Within 24 hours, double read
Before you come
For contrast scans: no food for 4 hours, water is fine, and a kidney blood test from the last 3 months. Bring your diabetes medicine list.

Digital X-ray

The quickest first look at bones, joints and the chest. Walk in with a request form during opening hours, no appointment needed.

In the room
About 10 minutes
Report
Same day, often within the hour
Before you come
Nothing to prepare. Tell the radiographer if you might be pregnant.

Ultrasound and echo

Sound waves, no radiation. Used for the abdomen, thyroid, blood vessels, tendons and, as an echocardiogram, the heart.

On the couch
20 to 30 minutes, 40 for an echo
Report
Same day
Before you come
Abdomen: no food for 6 hours. Pelvis: drink a litre of water an hour before and do not empty your bladder.

Before your scan, in one page.

We send these notes by WhatsApp when you book, in English or Arabic. If anything is unclear, call the department directly on 2449 7140.

MRI

Arrive 20 minutes early

  • Complete the metal safety form; we check it with you
  • Pacemaker, cochlear implant or clips? Call us before the day
  • Remove jewellery, hairpins and make-up with metallic pigment
  • Feeling anxious? Ask for a feet-first scan or a companion in the room

CT with contrast

Arrive 30 minutes early

  • No food for 4 hours; keep drinking water
  • Bring a kidney function result from the last 3 months
  • Taking metformin? We tell you if it needs to pause for 48 hours
  • Tell us about any previous reaction to contrast

Ultrasound

Arrive 15 minutes early

  • Abdomen: no food for 6 hours beforehand
  • Pelvis: one litre of water an hour before, full bladder
  • Thyroid, vessels and joints: no preparation
  • Women sonographers are available on request

X-ray

Walk in, no appointment

  • Bring the request form from your doctor
  • Wear clothes without zips or buttons over the area
  • Tell the radiographer if you could be pregnant
  • Most patients are done in under 20 minutes

Every MRI and CT is read by two radiologists.

A second pair of eyes catches what a single tired reader can miss. It costs us more time per scan. We think it is the most important thing we do.

  1. 1

    Quality check

    The radiographer checks every sequence before you leave the table, so you are not called back.

  2. 2

    First read

    A subspecialty radiologist reports: neuro, musculoskeletal, cardiac or body imaging.

  3. 3

    Second read

    A second radiologist reviews the images blind to the first report. Differences go to a joint review.

  4. 4

    Report released

    To you on a secure link and WhatsApp, and to your doctor, with an Arabic summary.
