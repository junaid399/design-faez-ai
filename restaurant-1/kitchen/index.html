No dining room. Nothing to hide.

Sufra runs two delivery-only kitchens in Riyadh. 1,150 square metres in Al Malqa, 820 in Al Rawdah. This is how a bag gets from a crate of tomatoes to your door.

Jump to the floor plan

The Al Malqa floor, one way through.

Raw food comes in on the left and cooked food leaves on the right. Nothing walks backwards, which is the simplest way to keep raw and ready apart.

  1. 1Every crate is probed and logged before it crosses the door.
  2. 2Raw proteins sit on the bottom shelves, ready food above.
  3. 3Prep runs on four board colours: raw meat, poultry, fish, vegetables.
  4. 4Five stations cook in parallel off one split ticket.
  5. 5Expo checks each item, seals the bag and stamps the time.
  6. 6Riders collect from a shaded bay without entering the kitchen.

A day on the line, from crates to close.

Two shifts, one handover, twenty-one hours of the clock with someone in whites.

  1. 06:00

    Deliveries

    Produce from Qassim and Al Kharj farms arrives. Every crate probed and logged at the door.

  2. 08:30

    Prep

    Sauces, marinades, dough balls and cut vegetables, each labelled with prep and use-by times.

  3. 10:45

    Line check

    Station leads sign off temperatures, stock levels and the allergen board.

  4. 13:00

    Lunch rush

    Green Hour and Sahn Rice Co take a third of all orders. Cold rail at full staff.

  5. 15:30

    Deep clean

    Fryer oil tested, hoods wiped and floors scrubbed between rushes.

  6. 17:00

    Handover

    Night shift reads the day log, then takes over station by station.

  7. 22:00

    Peak hour

    Up to 212 orders an hour across both kitchens. Every station doubled.

  8. 01:00

    Late menu

    Wing Shift and Kunafa Lab carry the night while the grill winds down.

  9. 03:00

    Close and sanitise

    Last bag out around 3:10, full sanitise until 4:30, logs locked.
