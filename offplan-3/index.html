Mirath means inheritance. What Najd left us is not a style but a set of rules: build thick, open small, face inward, and let the courtyard do the cooling.

The wadi cliffs and the parapet line: the view that set our roof heights.

At-Turaif, the mud-brick capital of the first Saudi state and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010, stands a short drive up Wadi Hanifah. Its houses were cool in July without a single machine. We studied how.

Mirath keeps those rules and drops the nostalgia. Inside, you get double-glazed openings, VRF air conditioning, lifts in every building and kitchens built for real family cooking. Outside, you get walls you can read from the street as Najdi.

How a courtyard
cools a house.

A section through Building 3 at 2 pm in July. The court is deeper than it is wide, so its floor sits in shade for most of the day and holds the night air until late morning.

Swipe to see the whole section

  1. Thick walls. 45 cm insulated block with lime plaster delays the afternoon heat by about eight hours.
  2. Small, deep openings. Windows face the court, not the street, set back 30 cm so the glass stays in shadow.
  3. A deep court. 1.2 times deeper than it is wide, so the floor sees direct sun for under two hours a day in summer.
  4. Night air reservoir. Cool air sinks into the court after Isha and is drawn into ground-floor rooms until late morning.
  5. High vents. Triangular openings under the parapet let warm air leave each room without opening a window.

Five materials,
each chosen for heat.

A short palette from the ground around Riyadh. Nothing is there for decoration alone.

  • Lime and earth plaster طين

    Facades and courtyard walls. Breathes, and never needs paint.

  • Riyadh limestone حجر

    Plinths, courtyard floors and steps, cut 40 km from site.

  • Athl timber أثل

    Entrance doors and window shutters, oiled, not lacquered.

  • Palm rib ceilings جريد

    Majlis and roof pergolas: shade with gaps for the breeze.

  • Stepped brick screens لبن

    Stair cores and roof walls, open to air, closed to view.

Who can buy at Mirath?

Saudi citizens, GCC nationals and premium residency holders can buy directly. Other residents can buy one home for their own use under the current ownership rules; our team checks eligibility before you reserve.

What does the Wafi licence protect?

Your payments go into an escrow account, are released only against certified progress, and the project cannot be sold without approved land title, permits and designs. If the project stops, escrow funds are protected for buyers.

Can I use a mortgage?

Yes. Most Saudi banks finance off-plan homes on Wafi projects. Buyers usually pay the first 60% from savings during construction and finance the 40% handover payment.

Is there air conditioning, or only passive cooling?

Both. Every home has VRF air conditioning. The courtyard design simply means it runs less: the thermal model shows a cooling load about 27% below a standard apartment of the same size.

How private are the courtyards?

Each court is shared by 24 to 30 homes in one building only. Windows into the court are offset floor to floor, so no two windows face each other directly.

What are the service charges?

Estimated at SAR 18 per m² a year, covering courtyard and garden care, security, lifts and the shaded basement parking. The final rate is set by the owners association after handover.

When can I visit?

The sales gallery on Wadi Hanifah Road has a full-size mock-up of a Hosh living room and a courtyard corner. It is open Saturday to Thursday, 10 am to 9 pm.
